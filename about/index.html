Food waste starts before the bin

Every day a school kitchen decides what to cook, how much to prepare and how big each portion is. It decides from nutrition rules and expected ingredients, without knowing what students actually eat. By the time anyone notices, the food is already in the bin.

The food isn’t good today.

What we heard in our own canteen as classmates left their meals half-eaten. We wondered: does the kitchen ever hear that? A kitchen manager we interviewed confirmed it does not. Student feedback and leftovers are not part of how menus are planned.

One loop, a better meal every week

FoodLoop turns student votes and daily leftover data into small, tested menu changes. Nutrition is a guardrail: no change is suggested if it makes the meal less healthy.

  1. Plan

    The kitchen sets the menu, quantities and portion sizes for the week.

  2. Listen

    Students give a quick vote after lunch and choose a portion. The kitchen logs leftovers in a short daily form.

  3. Understand

    AI connects votes, comments and leftovers by dish and day to find why food is left.

  4. Simulate

    Before cooking, compare portion, recipe, preparation and serving changes on waste, acceptance, nutrition and cost.

  5. Experiment

    Test one change with a small group instead of the whole school.

  6. Measure

    Record what really happened: leftovers, satisfaction and nutrition.

  7. Learn

    The result is fed back, so the next suggestion fits this school better.

  8. Improve

    Roll out what worked and tell students: “You said → We changed”.

Every result feeds the next decision. The loop is the product.

What the AI actually does

A kitchen cannot connect hundreds of votes, comments and leftover logs by hand every day. FoodLoop’s AI does that work and turns it into one clear reason and the next best change.

Reads student comments

Groups free-text comments into themes such as taste, portion and temperature, and links them to each dish.

In: comments · Out: themes per dish

Finds the real cause

Separates a taste problem from a portion problem by connecting votes, comments and leftovers.

In: votes, comments, leftovers · Out: likely cause

Ranks the next change

Compares options by expected waste and acceptance, and rejects any that break nutrition rules.

In: options, nutrition rules · Out: ranked changes

Gets smarter every test

Each experiment result is fed back, so suggestions become specific to that school’s students and menu.

In: test results · Out: better next suggestion

What we will measure

Pilot targets, not results yet. Measured against a 1-week baseline at the pilot school.
